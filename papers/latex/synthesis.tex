\begin{abstract}
We synthesize three reconstructions of finite-dimensional classical mechanics and test the
founding thesis that physics studies empirically realized compositional mathematical
structures.  A Hamiltonian theory object must include not only $(M,\omega,H)$, but selected
preparations, observables, processes, decomposition and a realization map.  This enrichment
prevents mathematical isomorphism from being mistaken for physical equivalence.  It also
exposes four obstructions: canonical relations compose only partially; interaction is not an
invariant of $(M,\omega,H)$; symplectic Hamiltonian systems are not closed under ordinary
interconnection; and finite-precision observational equivalence is neither transitive nor a
congruence.  Exact data do not in general identify a unique Hamiltonian.  Consequently the
founding thesis is not itself a falsifiable physical hypothesis.  Its useful residue is an
explicit empirical contract whose compositional clauses can fail.  We retain all assumptions,
counterexamples, domain restrictions, and unsettled novelty claims rather than absorbing them
into a slogan.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, scope, and epistemic discipline}

The pilot question is whether finite-dimensional Hamiltonian mechanics can be represented as
an empirically interpreted compositional theory object without conflating mathematical and
physical equivalence.  Field theory, general relativity, quantum theory and infinite-dimensional
analysis are boundary tests only.  The mathematical foundations are standard
\cite{AbrahamMarsden1978,Arnold1989}; the synthesis joins the three topic studies
\cite{PilotI,PilotII,PilotIII}.

\begin{assumption}[Smooth finite scope]\label{asm:scope}
State spaces are finite-dimensional, second-countable smooth manifolds.  Vector fields are
locally Lipschitz; either their relevant flows are complete or a finite observation horizon is
fixed.  These conditions exclude genuine failures: Newtonian many-body dynamics can develop
noncollision singularities \cite{Xia1992}, while non-Lipschitz forces need not determine unique
trajectories \cite{Norton2008}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{axiom}[Epistemic separation]\label{ax:ep}
Definitions fix vocabulary; axioms and assumptions are inputs; mathematical claims require
proof; empirical statements require an instrument and data; established physical results
require citations; interpretations do not inherit theorem status; conjectures remain open.
No mathematical equivalence is a physical equivalence until preparations, observables, units,
domains and measurement maps have been shown to correspond.
\end{axiom}

\begin{interpretation}
Axiom~\ref{ax:ep} is methodological, not a theorem about nature.  It is the device that keeps
the phrase ``empirically realized'' from laundering assumptions into results.
\end{interpretation}

\section{The reconstructed theory object}

\begin{definition}[Empirically interpreted compositional system]\label{def:ecs}
An object is a tuple
\[
 \mathbb T=(M,\omega,H,\mathcal A,S,\Oc,\mathcal P,\mathcal D,I),
\]
where $(M,\omega)$ is symplectic, $H\in\Cinf(M)$, $\mathcal A\subseteq\Cinf(M)$ is a
Poisson algebra, $S\subseteq M$ is a preparation class, $\Oc\subseteq\mathcal A$ is the
measured subfamily, $\mathcal P$ is a chosen process class, $\mathcal D$ is decomposition and
interconnection data, and $I$ maps prepared trajectories to possible finite records under an
instrument specification.  Hamiltonian evolution obeys
\(\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH\), \(\dot f=\{f,H\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of Hamiltonian evolution]\label{lem:nat}
If $\varphi:(M,\omega)\to(N,\eta)$ is symplectic and $H=K\circ\varphi$, then
$T\varphi\,X_H=X_K\circ\varphi$ and, wherever defined,
$\varphi\circ\Phi_H^t=\Phi_K^t\circ\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every $v$, $\eta(T\varphi X_H,T\varphi v)=\omega(X_H,v)=dH(v)
=dK(T\varphi v)$.  Nondegeneracy gives the vector-field identity; uniqueness of integral
curves gives the flow identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The bare triple forgets empirical structure]\label{prop:forget}
The functor that forgets $(\mathcal A,S,\Oc,\mathcal P,\mathcal D,I)$ is not faithful to
physical distinctions: one triple can support inequivalent measured quantities, apparatus
couplings and subsystem decompositions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On $(\R^2,dq\wedge dp)$ the symplectic map $(q,p)\mapsto(2q,p/2)$ conjugates suitable
Hamiltonians by Lemma~\ref{lem:nat}.  If each laboratory nevertheless reads its first coordinate,
the pointer values differ by a factor two.  Thus conjugacy of the bare triples does not fix the
realization map.  Likewise a linear canonical change on $\R^4$ can turn two uncoupled equal
oscillators into coordinates in which the same Hamiltonian contains cross terms; the triple
does not select which variables denote parts \cite{PilotI}.
\end{proof}

\begin{empirical}[Units and access]
Laboratories measure bounded-range, finite-resolution pointer records, not arbitrary elements
of $\Cinf(M)$.  Preparations occupy regions rather than mathematical points.  Treating every
smooth function and every point as physically accessible is therefore an idealizing assumption,
not an established fact.
\end{empirical}

\section{Composition: what works and what fails}

\begin{established}[Monoidal product]
Symplectic manifolds admit the symmetric monoidal product
$(M,\omega)\otimes(N,\eta)=(M\times N,\omega\oplus\eta)$ with one-point unit.  For
noninteracting Hamiltonians, $H\otimes K=H\circ\mathrm{pr}_M+K\circ\mathrm{pr}_N$
\cite{AbrahamMarsden1978}.
\end{established}

\begin{theorem}[The symplectic product is not cartesian]\label{thm:notcart}
If $\dim M=2n>0$ and $\dim N=2m>0$, neither the graph of
$\mathrm{pr}_M:M\times N\to M$ nor the graph of $x\mapsto(x,x)$ is a canonical relation of
the required type.  Hence the monoidal product supplies neither deletion nor copying.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The projection graph has dimension $2n+2m$, whereas a Lagrangian in
$(M\times N)^-\times M$ has dimension $2n+m$; its pulled-back form is $-\eta\ne0$.
The diagonal graph has dimension $2n$, whereas a Lagrangian in $M^-\times M\times M$ has
dimension $3n$; its pulled-back form is $-\omega+\omega+\omega=\omega\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{counterexample}[Canonical relations do not always compose]\label{cex:relations}
Canonical relations compose by a fibre product.  Without clean intersection and an appropriate
projection, the fibre product may be singular or its image nonembedded.  Explicit generating
functions yield a line with a half-ray attached at the origin, which is not a submanifold
\cite{PilotI}.  Thus $\SympRel$ is only a partially defined ``category''
\cite{Weinstein1982,Weinstein2010}.
\end{counterexample}

\begin{newresult}[Interaction needs empirically supplied decomposition]\label{new:interaction}
Within this reconstruction, ``the two subsystems interact'' is not an invariant of
$(M,\omega,H)$.  It is a predicate of the enriched pair $((M,\omega,H),\mathcal D)$.
\end{newresult}
\begin{proof}
For equal oscillators, normal-mode canonical coordinates diagonalize a spring-coupled Hamiltonian.
Relative to particle coordinates it contains a cross term and is called interacting; relative to
normal modes the same Hamiltonian is a sum and the flow is a product.  Since a symplectic
automorphism connects the descriptions, no invariant of the bare triple distinguishes them.
The designation becomes determinate only when $\mathcal D$ is tied to preparation and readout.
\end{proof}

\begin{established}[Closure requires a larger class]\label{est:closure}
Autonomous symplectic Hamiltonian systems are not closed under common physical interconnections.
Nonholonomic constraints, dampers, variable-rank constraints and singular reduction respectively
produce almost-Poisson dynamics, volume contraction, rank-changing presymplectic geometry and
stratified spaces \cite{GotayNesterHinds1978,KoonMarsden1997,SjamaarLerman1991}.  Dirac and
port-Hamiltonian structures provide a broader class closed under regular power-conserving
interconnection \cite{Courant1990,Cervera2007}; singularity hypotheses still matter.
\end{established}

\begin{corollary}
No single operation in the present finite-dimensional symplectic category is simultaneously
total, expressive of arbitrary interaction, and closed on its objects.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The direct product is total but represents noninteraction unless extra $H_{\rm int}$ is supplied;
canonical-relation composition is expressive but partial by Counterexample~\ref{cex:relations};
physical interconnection leaves the class by Established result~\ref{est:closure}.
\end{proof}

\section{Empirical realization and equivalence}

\begin{definition}[Realization]\label{def:real}
An instrument is $\Rc=(T,\Delta t,S,\Oc,\sigma,\eps_{\rm read},D)$: duration, sampling
interval, preparations, measured observables, preparation resolution, readout precision and
bounded range.  Its realization map assigns to each nominal preparation the set of sampled
records generated from its $\sigma$-neighbourhood.  A model is realized on a record when some
allowed prediction lies within the stated tolerance and inside $D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Observational tolerance]\label{def:tol}
For models sharing preparation and observable labels, let $\beta_\Oc:f\mapsto f'$ and
$\beta_S:s\mapsto s'$ be the label-matching bijections that carry each observable and each
preparation of $\Mc_1$ to the identically labelled one of $\Mc_2$ (as in Paper~III
\cite{PilotIII}, Definition~3.1), and put
\[
d_{T,\Oc,S}(\Mc_1,\Mc_2)=\sup_{s\in S,t\in[0,T],f\in\Oc}
 |f(\Phi^t_{H_1}(s))-f'(\Phi^t_{H_2}(s'))|.
\]
Write $\Mc_1\approx_\eps\Mc_2$ when $d_{T,\Oc,S}\le\eps$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-precision equivalence is a tolerance relation]\label{thm:tolerance}
$\approx_\eps$ is reflexive and symmetric but, for every $\eps>0$ on ordinary continuous
model families, need not be transitive.  Taking its transitive closure can identify the entire
family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The displayed supremum is a pseudometric.  On $H_g=p^2/2+gq$, prepared at $(0,0)$ and
measured by $q$ for duration $T$, one has
$d(\Mc_g,\Mc_{g'})=|g-g'|T^2/2$.  Choose three equally spaced $g_i$ with adjacent distance
$\eps$ and endpoint distance $2\eps$.  Subdivision joins any two $g$ values by finitely many
$\eps$-steps, so the transitive closure is total.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite equivalence horizon]\label{prop:horizon}
If $X$ is $L$-Lipschitz, $\|X-Y\|\le\delta$, and paired trajectories remain in the certified
domain, then
\[
 |x(t)-y(t)|\le \frac{\delta}{L}(e^{Lt}-1),\qquad
 T_\eps=\frac{1}{L}\log(1+L\eps/\delta).
\]
The estimate is sharp for a Hamiltonian saddle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality is Gronwall's estimate \cite{Gronwall1919}.  For
$H=Lqp$ and $K=Lqp+\delta p$, trajectories from the origin differ in $q$ by exactly
$\delta(e^{Lt}-1)/L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite data underdetermine the Hamiltonian]\label{thm:under}
Let finitely many trajectories of $H_0$ be observed on a finite horizon, even continuously,
at infinite precision and through every smooth observable.  The consistent Hamiltonians contain
an infinite-dimensional affine family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite union $\Gamma$ of trajectory segments is compact and has empty interior.  Choose a
chart ball $B$ disjoint from $\Gamma$.  Every $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B)$ has $d\chi=0$ on
$\Gamma$, so $X_{H_0+\chi}=X_{H_0}$ there.  Uniqueness makes the observed trajectories and
all their observable values identical.  Since $C_c^\infty(B)$ is infinite-dimensional, so is
the consistent fibre.
\end{proof}

\begin{counterexample}[Equivalence is not structural identity]
An oscillator on $\R^2$ and the same visible oscillator with an unprepared, unmeasured second
mode on $\R^4$ produce identical records for all times and precisions, yet their state spaces
have different dimensions and are not diffeomorphic.  Conversely, Proposition~\ref{prop:forget}
shows that a symplectic isomorphism need not preserve fixed laboratory pointers.
\end{counterexample}

\begin{newresult}[Observational tolerance is not a congruence]\label{new:nocong}
Part-level observational equivalence need not survive interconnection: coupling can enlarge the
reachable set into a region on which two previously indistinguishable Hamiltonians differ.
\end{newresult}
\begin{proof}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:under}, place $\operatorname{supp}\chi$ outside the isolated observed orbit.
Paper~III \cite{PilotIII} supplies the explicit witness: the observed part is the unit-frequency
oscillator prepared at $(1,0)$ with orbit the unit circle, $\chi$ is supported in the ball of
radius $0.3$ about $(2,0)$, and the driver is a second unit-frequency oscillator prepared at
$(4,0)$ coupled by $H_{\rm int}=\kappa q_Aq_B$ with $\kappa=0.01$.  Resonant bilinear coupling
transfers amplitude over a beat of period $\pi/\kappa$, carrying the composite trajectory into
$\operatorname{supp}\chi$.  The two
isolated parts remain exactly equivalent, while the composite vector fields differ by
$X_\chi\neq0$, so the composite discrepancy is bounded below by a positive constant.  We do
\emph{not} claim it can be driven to infinity uniformly by scaling $\chi$: scaling changes the
dynamics inside the support and hence the dwell time, and that uniform estimate is proved
neither here nor in Paper~III.  The novelty of
this precise reachability formulation is claimed with moderate confidence; a direct antecedent
would require downgrading it to an established result.
\end{proof}

\section{Failure modes and falsifiable content}

\begin{definition}[Four failure modes]\label{def:fail}
For a model, instrument and record: \textbf{F1} is precision failure, a prediction missing the
record within its certified domain; \textbf{F2} is domain failure, where a trajectory or pointer
leaves the certified range; \textbf{F3} is idealization failure, where a claimed limiting family
does not converge uniformly on the relevant compact set and horizon; \textbf{F4} is
reconstruction degeneracy, where multiple models fit.  F4 includes off-support freedom,
level-set reparametrization and hidden structural degrees of freedom.
\end{definition}

\begin{counterexample}[Hamiltonicity depends on interpretation]\label{cex:damped}
For $m\ddot q=-kq-\gamma m\dot q$, identifying $p=m\dot q$ gives divergence $-\gamma$, so
no autonomous canonical Hamiltonian generates the measured $(q,p)$ flow.  With
$P=e^{\gamma t}m\dot q$, however,
$H=e^{-\gamma t}P^2/(2m)+e^{\gamma t}kq^2/2$ generates the same second-order equation.  The
time-dependent change scales the symplectic form and is not canonical.  Thus ``is Hamiltonian''
is not a property of an un-interpreted differential equation.
\end{counterexample}

\begin{empirical}
At $\gamma=0.1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, the physical $(q,p)$ phase-volume factor after $100$ seconds
is $e^{-10}\approx4.5\times10^{-5}$, whereas an autonomous Hamiltonian flow preserves that
volume.  Given calibrated phase-space reconstruction, this contrast is experimentally decidable.
\end{empirical}

\begin{interpretation}[Status of the founding thesis]\label{int:status}
``Physics is the study of empirically realized compositional mathematical structures'' is a
definition-like characterization, not a falsifiable empirical proposition: interpreted broadly,
any successful physical model can be called realized and some operation can be called
composition.  The falsifiable residue is narrower: a specified class of systems and processes
forms a stated symmetric monoidal structure; empirical realization preserves that structure;
and the resulting model predicts records under a declared auxiliary bundle.  Theorem
\ref{thm:notcart}, Established result~\ref{est:closure}, and New result~\ref{new:nocong} are
counterexample conditions for those clauses.
\end{interpretation}

\begin{conjecture}[Open reconstruction claim]
For generic finite records and positive tolerance, the consistent set has nonempty interior in
an appropriate $C^k$ topology.  Theorem~\ref{thm:under} does not prove this: it treats finitely
many finite-horizon trajectories and constructs off-support perturbations, but supplies neither
genericity nor a meagreness statement.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Synthesis and research contract}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal empirical contract]\label{prop:contract}
A scientifically usable compositional claim in this scope must state:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the object and process classes, including regularity and completeness domains;
\item the proposed product or interconnection and all extra interaction data;
\item the preparation, observable, unit, pointer and error correspondences;
\item the sense of equivalence and its dependence on $(\eps,T,\Oc,S)$;
\item closure, clean-intersection and reduction hypotheses;
\item failure criteria separating mismatch, domain exit, singular limit and nonidentifiability.
\end{enumerate}
Without these entries, ``empirically realized compositional structure'' has no determinate test.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Items 1 and 5 block the failures in Assumption~\ref{asm:scope}, Counterexample
\ref{cex:relations}, and Established result~\ref{est:closure}.  Item 2 is required by New
result~\ref{new:interaction}.  Items 3 and 4 are required by Proposition~\ref{prop:forget} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:tolerance}.  Item 6 distinguishes the logically different remedies encoded in
Definition~\ref{def:fail}.  Omitting any item leaves at least one exhibited counterexample
unclassified.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Corrected thesis]
Within the pilot scope, the defensible conclusion is: finite-dimensional classical physics uses
mathematical structures together with empirically supplied compositional and realization data;
neither datum is determined by the Hamiltonian triple, and both remain subject to explicit
failure tests.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Limitations retained]
We have not produced a novel empirical prediction, a total category of canonical relations, a
universal interconnection operation, or a uniqueness theorem for reconstruction.  Long-range
forces, dissipation, singular constraints and incomplete flows defeat common idealizations.
The two claims labelled new are mathematically supported here and in the topic papers, but
literature priority for their exact formulations remains less certain than their correctness.
\end{remark}
