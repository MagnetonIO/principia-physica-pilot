\title{Empirical Realization of Compositional Structure:\\
A Synthesis of the PRINCIPIA PHYSICA Topic Series}
\author{PRINCIPIA PHYSICA}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Three manuscripts of this series treat compositional modelling, Hamiltonian
reconstruction, and the empirical limits of structural physics. This paper
places them in one frame. We isolate a single mechanism --- a linear observation
operator with nonzero kernel --- and prove that the finite-sample
non-identifiability of Hamiltonians and the general finite-data
underdetermination theorem are two instances of it. We prove that a monoidal
natural transformation between compositional models is invisible to every closed
experiment, so that the empirical content of such a model is a gauge class
rather than a functor. We identify a third gap, distinct from finite data and
from gauge: a local-to-global obstruction measured by first cohomology, of which
the non-existence of a global Hamiltonian on the torus and the Aharonov--Bohm
phase are structurally parallel instances. We prove that a gauge constraint can
destroy tensor factorization outright, answering a standing objection to
compositional modelling with a proof rather than a qualification, and record the
Groenewold--van Hove theorem as a no-go on functorial transfer between theories.
Finally we prove a bound for propagating tolerance through composition, with a
counterexample fixing its scope to deterministic composites. The mathematical
core is machine-checked in Lean~4 and executed in Haskell. Nothing proved here
entails that any model is physically realized, and the paper is organized so
that the point at which that entailment fails is explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{What the series established, and what it did not}
\label{sec:intro}

The thesis of the treatise is that physics studies empirically realized
compositional mathematical structures, and the three manuscripts of this series
develop the two halves of that phrase separately on purpose. The compositional
paper defines a \emph{model} as a strong symmetric monoidal functor from an
interface theory to a system theory (C1) and proves that a model preserves every
equation generated by sequential and parallel composition (C2). The Hamiltonian
paper proves the regular Legendre correspondence (C3) and that finite sampled
trajectories do not pin down an unrestricted Hamiltonian off the sampled set
(C4). The empirical-limits paper proves a general underdetermination theorem for
classes closed under perturbations vanishing on the tested inputs (C5). The
series then records an interpretation (C6): admissibility supplies candidates,
realization requires separate evidence.

Read as a sequence these look like three unrelated cautionary results. This
paper argues they are not. Sections~\ref{sec:frame} and~\ref{sec:kernel} show
that C4 and C5 are one theorem applied to two observation operators, the
reduction being exact rather than analogical. Section~\ref{sec:gauge} shows that
a second, logically independent form of non-identifiability --- gauge --- is
present in all three settings, and proves the categorical instance the
compositional paper left implicit. Section~\ref{sec:global} identifies a third
form that none of the papers isolated and that is invisible to the other two.
Section~\ref{sec:nogo} records a no-go theorem on transferring compositional
structure between theories, and Section~\ref{sec:compose} supplies the one
positive quantitative result the series lacked.
Sections~\ref{sec:ledger}--\ref{sec:open} state the resulting four-status
ledger, report what has been machine-checked, and record disagreements and open
problems without resolving them.

Epistemic labels are used as in the series: a \emph{definition} stipulates; an
\emph{assumption} is granted without proof and carried into everything depending
on it; a \emph{theorem}, \emph{proposition}, \emph{lemma}, or \emph{corollary}
is proved here; an \emph{established physical result} is attributed and not
reproved; an \emph{empirical statement} has measurement as its warrant; an
\emph{interpretation} is a marked reading that no proof here supports; a
\emph{conjecture} is open.

\section{One frame for the three papers}
\label{sec:frame}

The three manuscripts share an epistemic pattern, but not a single ambient
mathematical type. The reconstruction and finite-record arguments use linear
model spaces and observation operators. The compositional argument instead
uses categories and functors, which need not carry any natural real-vector-space
structure. We therefore state the two vocabularies separately and connect them
only when an application explicitly supplies both.

\begin{definition}[Model class and observation operator]
\label{def:obs}
Let $V$ be a real vector space, $\mathcal L\subseteq V$ a \emph{linear-model class},
$D$ a real vector space of \emph{data}, and $O:V\to D$ a linear map, the
\emph{observation operator}. The \emph{record} of $m\in\mathcal L$ is $O(m)$.
\end{definition}

Linearity of $O$ is a restriction, and it is the one that makes the next section
work. It holds for the operators used in the linear portions of the series: restriction to a tested
set, evaluation of a differential at sampled points, and evaluation of a finite
family of linear functionals. It fails for a nonlinear noise model, and
Section~\ref{sec:open} records that gap. No claim is made that strong monoidal
functors form such a subset $\mathcal L$ or admit such an $O$.

\begin{definition}[Interface theory, system theory, compositional model]
\label{def:model}
An \emph{interface theory} is a symmetric monoidal category
$(\mathcal E,\otimes,I,\sigma)$ whose morphisms are admissible experimental
processes; a \emph{system theory} is a symmetric monoidal category
$(\mathcal M,\boxtimes,\mathbf 1)$; a \emph{compositional model} is a strong
symmetric monoidal functor $F:\mathcal E\to\mathcal M$ with structure
isomorphisms $\phi^F_{X,Y}:FX\boxtimes FY\to F(X\otimes Y)$ and
$\phi_0^F:\mathbf 1\to F(I)$. This is claim C1 of the series.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Substitution]
\label{lem:subst}
Let $F:\mathcal E\to\mathcal M$ be a functor and $e=e':X\to Y$ in $\mathcal E$.
Then for all composable $a:W\to X$ and $b:Y\to Z$,
\[
F(b\circ e\circ a)=F(b)\circ F(e)\circ F(a)=F(b)\circ F(e')\circ F(a).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equality of morphisms is a congruence for composition, so
$b\circ e\circ a=b\circ e'\circ a$ in $\mathcal E$. The assignment $F$ makes on
each hom-set a function, so equal arguments have equal images. The functor axiom
$F(g\circ f)=F(g)\circ F(f)$, applied twice, rewrites each image as the stated
composite in $\mathcal M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Contextual invariance]
\label{cor:context}
No context built from sequential composition separates the images under $F$ of
processes identified in $\mathcal E$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A sequential context is a morphism expression with one hole; filling the hole
with $e$ and with $e'$ produces the two sides of Lemma~\ref{lem:subst}.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:subst} and Corollary~\ref{cor:context} are machine-checked; see
Section~\ref{sec:formal}.

\begin{definition}[Closed experiment and normalized scalar]
\label{def:scalar}
A \emph{state} of $X$ is $s:I\to X$, an \emph{effect} is $p:X\to I$, and a
\emph{closed experiment} is a morphism $c\in\mathcal E(I,I)$; the experiments
$p\circ e\circ s$ are of this form. For a strong monoidal $F$, the
\emph{normalized scalar} of $c$ is
\[
\langle F,c\rangle \;=\; (\phi_0^F)^{-1}\circ F(c)\circ\phi_0^F
\;\in\;\mathcal M(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Probabilistic interpretation]
\label{ass:prob}
There is a map $\nu:\mathcal M(\mathbf 1,\mathbf 1)\to[0,1]$ such that the
scalars assigned to the mutually exclusive outcomes of each specified
measurement sum to one. This is granted, not proved; every probability
statement below inherits it.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility, identifiability, gauge]
\label{def:compat}
Fix tested inputs $T$, observed values $\widehat y_t$, and tolerances
$\delta_t\ge0$. The \emph{compatibility region} is
$C(T,\delta)=\{m\in\mathcal L:\ d(q_t(m),\widehat y_t)\le\delta_t\ \text{for all}\ t\in T\}$.
The class is \emph{identifiable} under $O$ when $O(m)=O(n)$ implies $m=n$, and
\emph{identifiable up to} $\approx$ when $O(m)=O(n)$ implies $m\approx n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of compatibility]
\label{prop:mono}
If $T'\subseteq T$ and $\delta_t\le\delta'_t$ for $t\in T'$, then
$C(T,\delta)\subseteq C(T',\delta')$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $m\in C(T,\delta)$ and $t\in T'$. Then $t\in T$, so
$d(q_t(m),\widehat y_t)\le\delta_t\le\delta'_t$. As $t$ was arbitrary in $T'$,
$m\in C(T',\delta')$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:mono} unifies the ``monotonicity of realization''
proposition of the compositional paper with the ``tolerance'' and
``restriction'' monotonicity propositions of the empirical-limits paper: the
three are one inequality read on two arguments. Both readings are
machine-checked (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).

\begin{example}[Finite stochastic systems]
\label{ex:stoch}
Objects are finite sets, morphisms are column-stochastic matrices, sequential
composition is matrix product, parallel composition is the Kronecker product,
and $\nu$ is the identity on $[0,1]$. The accompanying Haskell program builds
this model and checks that it preserves associativity, the interchange law, and
naturality of the symmetry, and that column-stochasticity is closed under both
compositions.
\end{example}

\section{The kernel theorem: C4 and C5 are one result}
\label{sec:kernel}

\begin{assumption}[$O$-closure at $m$]
\label{ass:closure}
There is a subset $K\subseteq\ker O$ with $0\in K$ and $m+K\subseteq\mathcal L$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel underdetermination]
\label{thm:kernel}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:closure}, every $h\in K$ satisfies $O(m+h)=O(m)$, and
the map $h\mapsto m+h$ is injective. Consequently the fibre
$\{m'\in\mathcal L: O(m')=O(m)\}$ has cardinality at least $|K|$. If
$K\neq\{0\}$ then $\mathcal L$ is not identifiable under $O$; if $K$ is infinite
then the record leaves infinitely many admissible models standing.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $h\in K$, linearity of $O$ gives $O(m+h)=O(m)+O(h)=O(m)+0=O(m)$, and
$m+h\in\mathcal L$ by Assumption~\ref{ass:closure}, so $m+h$ lies in the fibre.
If $m+h=m+h'$ then $h=h'$ by cancellation in $V$, so the map $h\mapsto m+h$ is
injective and the fibre has at least $|K|$ elements. If $K$ contains some
$h\neq0$ then $m+h\neq m$ while $O(m+h)=O(m)$, which contradicts
identifiability.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} is short because it is the whole content of both prior
non-identifiability results. We now exhibit the reductions.

\begin{corollary}[C5 is an instance]
\label{cor:c5}
Let $X$ be a set, $W$ a real vector space, $V=\{f:X\to W\}$, $T\subseteq X$ the
tested inputs, and $O=R_T$ the restriction $f\mapsto f|_T$. Then $R_T$ is
linear, $\ker R_T=\{h: h|_T=0\}$, and the perturbation-closure assumption of the
empirical-limits paper is exactly Assumption~\ref{ass:closure} for a subset
$K\subseteq\ker R_T$. The finite-data underdetermination theorem of that paper
is therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Restriction is linear because addition of functions is pointwise:
$(f+g)|_T=f|_T+g|_T$ and $(\lambda f)|_T=\lambda(f|_T)$. Its kernel is the set
of $h$ with $h(x)=0$ for all $x\in T$, which is the description of the admitted
perturbations in that paper. The conclusion of that paper --- that $m+h$ is
record-equivalent to $m$ and distinct from it --- is the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[C4 is an instance, and the two natural data types agree]
\label{cor:c4}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold of positive dimension,
$S=\{z_1,\dots,z_N\}\subset M$ finite, $V=C^\infty(M)$,
$D=\bigoplus_{k=1}^N T^*_{z_k}M$, and $O_{\mathrm d}(H)=(dH(z_k))_k$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $O_{\mathrm d}$ is linear;
\item the velocity operator $O_{X}(H)=(X_H(z_k))_k$ satisfies
      $O_{\mathrm d}=\bigl(\bigoplus_k\omega^\flat_{z_k}\bigr)\circ O_X$ with each
      $\omega^\flat_{z_k}$ a linear isomorphism, hence
      $\ker O_{\mathrm d}=\ker O_X$;
\item $\ker O_{\mathrm d}$ contains the infinite set $\{\lambda b:\lambda\in\mathbb R\}$
      for any nonzero smooth bump $b$ supported in an open ball disjoint
      from $S$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the finite-sample non-identifiability theorem of the Hamiltonian
paper is Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} applied to $O_{\mathrm d}$, and the same
conclusion holds verbatim for velocity data.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(1) Exterior differentiation is $\mathbb R$-linear on functions and evaluation
at a point is linear, so $O_{\mathrm d}$ is a composite of linear maps.
(2) With the convention $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, the bundle map
$\omega^\flat_z:T_zM\to T^*_zM$, $v\mapsto\iota_v\omega_z$, satisfies
$\omega^\flat_z(X_H(z))=dH(z)$; it is a linear isomorphism at each $z$ because
$\omega$ is nondegenerate. Applying it fibrewise gives the stated factorization,
and precomposition of an injective map preserves kernels, so the two kernels
coincide.
(3) A finite set in a positive-dimensional manifold has empty interior, so an
open coordinate ball $U$ disjoint from $S$ exists; take $b$ nonzero, smooth,
supported in $U$. Each $\lambda b$ vanishes identically on a neighbourhood of
every $z_k$, hence $d(\lambda b)(z_k)=0$ for all $k$, and distinct $\lambda$
give distinct functions because $b\neq0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Identification inside a finite linear class]
\label{cor:linear}
If $\mathcal L=\{\sum_{j=1}^m\theta_j\phi_j\}$ for fixed smooth $\phi_j$, then
$\theta$ is determined by the record if and only if the design map
$A\theta=(\sum_j\theta_j\,d\phi_j(z_k))_k$ has trivial kernel.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Two parameters give the same record exactly when their difference lies in
$\ker A$, and $\ker A=\{0\}$ is exactly the case $K=\{0\}$ excluded by
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:kernel}
Corollaries~\ref{cor:c5}--\ref{cor:linear} locate non-identifiability in the
pair (model class, observation operator) and nowhere else. Enlarging $T$ shrinks
$\ker R_T$, but a class closed under bump perturbations retains a nonzero kernel
for every finite $T$, so more data of the same kind is never the remedy. The
remedy is a restriction of the class --- a degree bound, a dictionary, a
smoothness scale --- which is a modelling commitment requiring its own warrant.
No theorem in this series supplies that warrant.
\end{interpretation}

\section{Gauge: what no record can see}
\label{sec:gauge}

Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} concerns a finite record. A second and independent
question is what remains invisible to an \emph{ideal, complete} record. We show
that all three papers exhibit this second phenomenon, and prove the categorical
case.

\begin{theorem}[Monoidal gauge invisibility]
\label{thm:gauge}
Let $F,G:\mathcal E\to\mathcal M$ be strong monoidal functors and let
$\alpha:F\Rightarrow G$ be a monoidal natural transformation, that is, a natural
transformation satisfying
$\alpha_{X\otimes Y}\circ\phi^F_{X,Y}=\phi^G_{X,Y}\circ(\alpha_X\boxtimes\alpha_Y)$
and $\alpha_I\circ\phi_0^F=\phi_0^G$. Then for every closed experiment
$c\in\mathcal E(I,I)$,
\[
\langle G,c\rangle=\langle F,c\rangle .
\]
No invertibility of $\alpha$ is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The unit axiom $\alpha_I\circ\phi_0^F=\phi_0^G$ and invertibility of
$\phi_0^F$ give $\alpha_I=\phi_0^G\circ(\phi_0^F)^{-1}$. Both $\phi_0^F$ and
$\phi_0^G$ are isomorphisms because $F$ and $G$ are strong, so $\alpha_I$ is an
isomorphism with $\alpha_I^{-1}=\phi_0^F\circ(\phi_0^G)^{-1}$. Naturality of
$\alpha$ at $c:I\to I$ is the equation
$\alpha_I\circ F(c)=G(c)\circ\alpha_I$, and composing on the right with
$\alpha_I^{-1}$ yields $G(c)=\alpha_I\circ F(c)\circ\alpha_I^{-1}$. Substituting
into Definition~\ref{def:scalar},
\[
\langle G,c\rangle
=(\phi_0^G)^{-1}\alpha_I\,F(c)\,\alpha_I^{-1}\phi_0^G
=(\phi_0^G)^{-1}\phi_0^G(\phi_0^F)^{-1}\,F(c)\,\phi_0^F(\phi_0^G)^{-1}\phi_0^G
=(\phi_0^F)^{-1}F(c)\,\phi_0^F
=\langle F,c\rangle. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The empirical content of a compositional model is a class]
\label{cor:gaugeclass}
Let $\approx$ be the equivalence relation on strong monoidal functors
$\mathcal E\to\mathcal M$ generated by ``there exists a monoidal natural
transformation from one to the other''. Then $F\approx G$ implies
$\langle F,c\rangle=\langle G,c\rangle$ for all closed $c$, and under
Assumption~\ref{ass:prob} the two models predict the same probability for every
closed experiment.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $F\sim G$ when $\langle F,c\rangle=\langle G,c\rangle$ for all closed $c$.
The relation $\sim$ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive because equality of
morphisms is. Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} shows that $\sim$ contains every
generating pair of $\approx$. An equivalence relation containing the generators
of $\approx$ contains $\approx$, because $\approx$ is by construction the
smallest such relation. The probability statement is the application of $\nu$ to
equal scalars, and depends on Assumption~\ref{ass:prob}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The Hamiltonian gauge is exactly the constants]
\label{prop:hamgauge}
Let $(M,\omega)$ be a connected symplectic manifold. If $H$ and $K$ generate the
same Hamiltonian vector field, then $H-K$ is constant; conversely adding a
constant leaves the field unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\iota_{X}\omega=dH$ and $\iota_{X}\omega=dK$ for the same $X$, then
$d(H-K)=0$. A smooth function with vanishing differential on a connected
manifold is constant. Conversely $d(H+c)=dH$ for constant $c$, and $\omega$ is
nondegenerate, so the generated fields coincide.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:hamgauge} is the continuous statement; its discrete
one-dimensional analogue --- the kernel of a forward-difference operator is
exactly the constants --- is machine-checked by induction in the accompanying
Lean development, in both directions.

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:twogaps}
Sections~\ref{sec:kernel} and~\ref{sec:gauge} isolate two logically independent
deficits. The kernel of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} shrinks as the tested set
grows; the gauge of Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} and Proposition~\ref{prop:hamgauge}
does not shrink at all, being a degeneracy of the map from mathematics to
predictions rather than a shortage of experiments. Conflating them produces two
familiar errors: treating a gauge freedom as an experimental target, and
treating a genuine data deficit as mere convention.
\end{interpretation}

\section{A third gap: local data, global structure}
\label{sec:global}

Neither of the previous two mechanisms accounts for the following situation:
complete, ideal, local information, and still no determination of the global
object. The obstruction is cohomological.

\begin{proposition}[Symplectic versus Hamiltonian]
\label{prop:cohom}
Let $X$ be a smooth vector field on a symplectic manifold $(M,\omega)$. Then
$\mathcal L_X\omega=0$ if and only if $\iota_X\omega$ is closed, and $X$ admits
a global Hamiltonian if and only if $\iota_X\omega$ is exact. The obstruction is
the class $[\iota_X\omega]\in H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)$, and it vanishes on every
contractible open set.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cartan's formula gives
$\mathcal L_X\omega=d(\iota_X\omega)+\iota_X(d\omega)$, and $d\omega=0$ because
$\omega$ is symplectic, so $\mathcal L_X\omega=d(\iota_X\omega)$; the first
equivalence is the vanishing of this expression. A global Hamiltonian is by
definition a function $H$ with $\iota_X\omega=dH$, which is exactness. The class
$[\iota_X\omega]$ is defined precisely when the form is closed and vanishes
precisely when it is exact. On a contractible open set the Poincar\'e lemma
makes every closed one-form exact, so the class vanishes there.
\end{proof}

\begin{counterexample}[Locally Hamiltonian, globally not]
\label{cex:torus}
On the two-torus with angular coordinates $(q,p)$ and $\omega=dq\wedge dp$, take
$X=\partial_p$. Then $\iota_X\omega=-dq$, which is closed but not exact, since
its integral around the $q$-cycle is $-2\pi\neq0$ while every exact form
integrates to zero over a cycle. Local Hamiltonians exist on every contractible
chart; no single-valued global one does.
\end{counterexample}

The physical counterpart of Counterexample~\ref{cex:torus} is not a
mathematical curiosity, and it is measured.

\begin{established}[Aharonov--Bohm]
\label{est:ab}
In a field-free but non-simply-connected region surrounding a confined magnetic
flux $\Phi$, the relative phase acquired by an electron beam split around the
flux is $e\Phi/\hbar$, and the resulting interference shift is observed. The
effect was predicted in \cite{AB}, reported in \cite{Chambers}, and confirmed
with the field completely shielded by a superconductor in \cite{Tonomura}. The
gauge-invariant description of the observable is the holonomy of the connection,
not the local field strength \cite{WuYang}.
\end{established}

\begin{empirical}[What Established result~\ref{est:ab} contributes here]
\label{emp:ab}
The local field strength vanishes identically on the accessible region while the
observable phase does not. Complete local data therefore fails to determine an
observable quantity --- an empirical statement, and the reason the corresponding
mathematical possibility cannot be dismissed as pathological.
\end{empirical}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:global}
Counterexample~\ref{cex:torus} and Established result~\ref{est:ab} are
structurally parallel: in each the datum is a closed one-form on a region with
$H^1\neq0$, and the undetermined quantity is its class. We assert the parallel
as a reading, not an identification: the two concern different physical content,
are established by different means --- one by proof, one by measurement --- and
neither is derived from the other here. What it does establish is that the
local-to-global gap is a third mechanism, independent of
Sections~\ref{sec:kernel} and~\ref{sec:gauge}: it survives complete local data,
and unlike gauge it concerns a quantity that is observed.
\end{interpretation}

A related objection is that gauge constraints can prevent decomposing a physical
state space into local tensor factors. It is correct, and the following makes it
precise.

\begin{proposition}[A constraint can destroy factorization]
\label{prop:nofactor}
Let $\mathcal H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2$ and let
$\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}=\{\psi\in\mathcal H:(Z\otimes Z)\psi=\psi\}$, where
$Z=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$. Then $\dim\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}=2$, and there are
no subspaces $V_1,V_2\subseteq\mathbb C^2$ with
$\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}=V_1\otimes V_2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the basis $\{\ket{00},\ket{01},\ket{10},\ket{11}\}$ the operator
$Z\otimes Z$ is diagonal with entries $1,-1,-1,1$, so its $+1$ eigenspace is
$\mathrm{span}\{\ket{00},\ket{11}\}$, of dimension $2$. Suppose
$\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}=V_1\otimes V_2$. Then
$\dim V_1\cdot\dim V_2=2$ with each $\dim V_i\le 2$, so one factor has dimension
$1$ and the other dimension $2$. Assume $\dim V_1=1$, say
$V_1=\mathbb C v$ with $v=\alpha\ket0+\beta\ket1\neq0$, and
$V_2=\mathbb C^2$; the other case is identical after exchanging the tensor
factors. Then $v\otimes w\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}$ for every
$w\in\mathbb C^2$. Writing $v\otimes w=\ket0\otimes(\alpha w)+\ket1\otimes(\beta w)$
and comparing with the general element $a\ket{00}+b\ket{11}$ of
$\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}$, membership forces $\alpha w\in\mathbb C\ket0$ and
$\beta w\in\mathbb C\ket1$. Take $w=\ket0+\ket1$, which is nonzero. Then
$\alpha w\in\mathbb C\ket0$ forces $\alpha=0$, and $\beta w\in\mathbb C\ket1$
forces $\beta=0$, so $v=0$, contradicting $v\neq0$. Hence no such $V_1,V_2$
exist.
\end{proof}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:nofactor}
Constraints of exactly the type in Proposition~\ref{prop:nofactor} --- a Gauss
law --- are what distinguishes a gauge theory's physical state space from the
tensor product of local kinematic spaces; the resulting difficulty for local
decomposition is documented in \cite{CHR}. The consequence for
Definition~\ref{def:model} is a scope restriction, not a refutation: a symmetric
monoidal structure on $\mathcal M$ guarantees that a tensor product
\emph{exists in the category} and guarantees nothing about whether a constrained
physical state space is such a product of local factors. Inferring the latter
from the former is what this treatise forbids, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:nofactor} shows the inference is not merely unjustified
but false.
\end{interpretation}

\section{A no-go on functorial transfer}
\label{sec:nogo}

Sections~\ref{sec:kernel}--\ref{sec:global} concern what a model fails to
determine. There is a further limit: structure-preserving maps \emph{between}
theories need not exist at all.

\begin{established}[Groenewold--van Hove]
\label{est:gvh}
There is no linear map $Q$ from the Poisson algebra of polynomials on
$\mathbb R^{2n}$ to self-adjoint operators satisfying all of
$Q(\{f,g\})=\tfrac{1}{i\hbar}[Q(f),Q(g)]$ for all polynomials $f,g$;
$Q(1)=\mathrm{Id}$; and irreducibility of the representation generated by
$Q(q^i),Q(p_i)$. The obstruction is present already at low degree. The result is
due to \cite{Groenewold,vanHove}; a modern statement and proof, with the precise
subalgebras on which quantization does and does not exist, is \cite{Gotay}.
\end{established}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:nogo}
We do not reprove Established result~\ref{est:gvh}, and draw one consequence for
Definition~\ref{def:model}. Compositional modelling is often defended on the
ground that a structure-preserving map between theories transports results
without loss; Established result~\ref{est:gvh} shows that the existence of such
a map is a substantive question with a negative answer in a central case.
Compositionality is therefore a constraint, not a convenience: it can be so
strong that nothing satisfies it. This qualifies claim C2, which is conditional
on a functor existing --- exactly what is denied for the classical-to-quantum
case in full generality.
\end{interpretation}

\begin{conjecture}[Characterizing admissible transfers]
\label{conj:transfer}
There is a characterization, in terms of the interface theory alone, of which
sub-theories of a classical Poisson theory admit a symmetric monoidal functor
into a quantum system theory preserving the stated brackets and irreducibility.
Partial results are known for specific subalgebras \cite{Gotay}; we state the
general question as open and claim no progress on it here.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Propagating tolerance through composition}
\label{sec:compose}

The series proves that composition preserves equations and that finite data
underdetermine models, but it supplies no quantitative statement about how a
realization claim for parts becomes a realization claim for the whole. The
following supplies one, and the counterexample that follows fixes its scope.

\begin{lemma}[Composition bound]
\label{lem:comp}
Let $(A,d_A),(B,d_B),(C,d_C)$ be metric spaces, let $S\subseteq A$, and let
$f_1,\widehat f_1:A\to B$ and $f_2,\widehat f_2:B\to C$. Assume
\begin{enumerate}
\item $d_B(f_1(a),\widehat f_1(a))\le\varepsilon_1$ for all $a\in S$;
\item $f_2$ has a nondecreasing modulus of continuity $\mu$, so that
      $d_B(b,b')\le t$ implies $d_C(f_2(b),f_2(b'))\le\mu(t)$;
\item $d_C(f_2(b),\widehat f_2(b))\le\varepsilon_2$ for all $b\in\widehat f_1(S)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for every $a\in S$,
\[
d_C\bigl(f_2(f_1(a)),\ \widehat f_2(\widehat f_1(a))\bigr)\ \le\ \mu(\varepsilon_1)+\varepsilon_2 .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $a\in S$ and insert the intermediate point $f_2(\widehat f_1(a))$. The
triangle inequality in $C$ gives
\[
d_C\bigl(f_2(f_1(a)),\widehat f_2(\widehat f_1(a))\bigr)
\le d_C\bigl(f_2(f_1(a)),f_2(\widehat f_1(a))\bigr)
 + d_C\bigl(f_2(\widehat f_1(a)),\widehat f_2(\widehat f_1(a))\bigr).
\]
Hypothesis (1) gives $d_B(f_1(a),\widehat f_1(a))\le\varepsilon_1$, so
hypothesis (2) bounds the first term by $\mu(\varepsilon_1)$. Hypothesis (3),
applied at the point $\widehat f_1(a)\in\widehat f_1(S)$, bounds the second term
by $\varepsilon_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lipschitz and chain forms]
\label{cor:chain}
If $\mu(t)=Lt$ the bound reads $L\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2$. For a chain
$f_n\circ\cdots\circ f_1$ compared with $\widehat f_n\circ\cdots\circ\widehat f_1$,
where $f_i$ is $L_i$-Lipschitz and stage $i$ has tolerance $\varepsilon_i$, the
composite tolerance is at most
\[
E_n\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}L_j\Bigr)\varepsilon_i .
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The Lipschitz case is Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} with $\mu(t)=Lt$. For the chain,
argue by induction on $n$. For $n=1$ the bound is $\varepsilon_1$, which is
hypothesis (1). Assume $E_{n-1}\le\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\prod_{j=i+1}^{n-1}L_j)\varepsilon_i$.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} with inner maps the two $(n-1)$-fold composites,
outer maps $f_n$ and $\widehat f_n$, and $\mu(t)=L_nt$ gives
$E_n\le L_nE_{n-1}+\varepsilon_n$. Substituting the inductive bound and
distributing $L_n$ across the sum produces
$\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}L_j)\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_n$, which is
the stated expression, the $i=n$ term having empty product $1$.
\end{proof}

Both bounds are met with equality on the examples in the accompanying Haskell
program, so a wrong constant would be detected rather than hidden by slack.
Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} is machine-checked in Lean in an integer modulus form.

\begin{proposition}[Components do not determine the composite]
\label{prop:joint}
There exist two joint distributions on $\{0,1\}^2$ with identical single-component
marginals and different probability for the event that the two outputs agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $P$ be uniform on $\{0,1\}^2$ and let $Q$ give probability $\tfrac12$ to
each of $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. Each marginal of $P$ is uniform, and each marginal
of $Q$ is uniform because $Q(\text{first}=0)=\tfrac12$ and
$Q(\text{second}=0)=\tfrac12$. The event that the outputs agree has probability
$\tfrac12$ under $P$ and $1$ under $Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Compositional closure]
\label{ass:closurecomp}
Predictions for a composite are determined by the component models together with
the declared interface, with no unmodelled shared cause, memory channel, or
context dependence across that interface.
\end{assumption}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:compose}
Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} propagates a \emph{deterministic} scope claim on metric
hypotheses alone. Proposition~\ref{prop:joint} shows that no analogue holds for
joint statistics without a further premise, the componentwise data being
identical in the two cases while a composite prediction differs. The premise
that closes the gap is Assumption~\ref{ass:closurecomp}, and it is empirical:
tests for cross-component correlation, memory, leakage, and contextual
dependence bear on it, whereas the availability of a tensor product in
$\mathcal M$ does not.
\end{interpretation}

\section{The four-status ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

The synthesis of the three papers is that a claim in structural physics occupies
exactly one of four statuses, and that the series' apparent variety of negative
results is the variety of ways of confusing them.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Status & Established by & Fails to give \\
\midrule
Mathematical admissibility & Proof inside a formal system & Any empirical claim \\
Finite-record compatibility & Data plus a stated tolerance & Uniqueness (Thm.~\ref{thm:kernel}) \\
Identifiability in a class & Trivial kernel (Cor.~\ref{cor:linear}) & Warrant for the class \\
Physical realization & Measurement, within a stated scope & Extrapolation beyond scope \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The four statuses. Each row takes a different kind of warrant, and no
upward inference is valid without an added premise stated here as an assumption.}
\label{tab:ledger}
\end{table}

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:ladder}
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} is the content of claim C6, refined. The negative results
distribute across the rows: Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} blocks the step from row
two to row three; Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge} and Proposition~\ref{prop:hamgauge}
show that row three is at best a statement about gauge classes;
Section~\ref{sec:global} shows that even ideal local data leaves row four
underdetermined when $H^1\neq0$; and Established result~\ref{est:gvh} shows that
row one can itself be empty for a demanded structure. So do the positive ones:
Lemma~\ref{lem:subst} lives in row one, Corollary~\ref{cor:linear} gives a
criterion for row three, and Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} propagates only a mathematical
compatibility bound for deterministic composites. It does not propagate row
four. Physical realization of a composite additionally requires
Assumption~\ref{ass:closurecomp}, including the empirical claim that connection
introduces no unmodelled back-reaction, loading, memory, or shared cause.
\end{interpretation}

\section{Formal artifacts and what they do and do not certify}
\label{sec:formal}

Two machine artifacts accompany this paper.

\texttt{formal/lean/Proofs.lean} is a Lean~4 development \cite{Lean4} using the
core library only, with no Mathlib dependency. It formalizes identifiability and
identifiability up to a gauge relation (Definition~\ref{def:compat}); record
congruence and the kernel theorem in finite-record form
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel}), with an explicit witness --- two integer-valued
models on $\mathbb N$ agreeing on the tested list $[0,1,2,3]$ and differing at
$7$; the statement that a difference operator's kernel is exactly the constants,
proved by induction in both directions (the discrete analogue of
Proposition~\ref{prop:hamgauge}); monotonicity of compatibility in the tolerance
and in the tested set (Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}); the composition bound in an
integer modulus form (Lemma~\ref{lem:comp}); and the substitution lemma with its
contextual-invariance corollary (Lemma~\ref{lem:subst},
Corollary~\ref{cor:context}). Lean 4.33.0 checks the file with no errors or
warnings. An axiom audit reports dependence on \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound} only, with the two categorical results depending on no
axioms; no result invokes \texttt{Classical.choice}.

\texttt{formal/haskell/Core.hs} is a Haskell program depending on \texttt{base}
only. It builds the stochastic-matrix model of Example~\ref{ex:stoch} and checks
functoriality; constructs the bump perturbation of Corollaries~\ref{cor:c5}
and~\ref{cor:c4} and confirms that it leaves the record and its first
derivatives on the tested set unchanged while separating the models by
$e^{-1}\approx0.3679$ off it; confirms that a degree bound restores
identification; checks the additive gauge of Proposition~\ref{prop:hamgauge};
integrates the harmonic oscillator with a St\"ormer--Verlet scheme, reporting a
maximum relative energy drift of $2.5\times10^{-5}$ over $4\times10^4$ steps;
performs the identification of Corollary~\ref{cor:linear} and confirms that the
degenerate design at $q=0$ is refused; checks Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}; and
verifies Lemma~\ref{lem:comp}, Corollary~\ref{cor:chain}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}, the first two with the bound attained exactly. It
type-checks under \texttt{-Wall} with no warnings; all $21$ checks pass.

\begin{interpretation}
\label{int:formal}
Machine checking certifies that the proofs of the stated \emph{mathematical}
propositions are correct. It certifies nothing about realization, and cannot:
the Lean development contains no predicate that could express realization, and
every numerical input to the Haskell program is a stipulated literal marked in
the source as synthetic. The final Lean theorem makes this explicit --- any
realization predicate constant on record-equivalence classes must assign the
same status to two models the record cannot separate, a statement about the
impotence of the formalism rather than a discovery about nature.
\end{interpretation}

\section{Recorded disagreements, failures, and open problems}
\label{sec:open}

Per the epistemic contract of the series, we record rather than resolve.

\paragraph{An unresolved review verdict.}
An independent review of the topic series returned the verdict \textsc{reject}
at confidence $0.95$, with three blocking findings: that the compositional
manuscript identified tensor admissibility with physical realization while
ignoring gauge constraints; that the Hamiltonian manuscript presented a
reconstruction existence claim without proof or citation; and that the
empirical-limits manuscript stated parameter scaling bounds as empirical facts
without empirical input. That review was directed at the drafts, and the revised
manuscripts address the second finding with a proved existence criterion. We
take no position on whether the revisions discharge the verdict, because the
review has not been rerun against them. What this paper adds is independent of
that question: Proposition~\ref{prop:nofactor} proves the substance of the first
finding rather than qualifying it, and this synthesis asserts no empirical
scaling bound of the kind at issue in the third.

\paragraph{Counterexamples supplied by review and retained.}
The review offered the Aharonov--Bohm effect and the Groenewold--van Hove
theorem as counterexamples to the series. Both are retained here as substantive
limits rather than deflected: the first as Established result~\ref{est:ab} and
Empirical statement~\ref{emp:ab}, the second as Established
result~\ref{est:gvh}. Neither is reproved here.

\paragraph{Failed constructions.}
Three attempts did not succeed. First, we found no proof that componentwise
realization implies composite realization, and Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}
shows none exists without Assumption~\ref{ass:closurecomp}. Second,
Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} has no counterpart for statistical models under total
variation or any other declared distance absent an independence or closure
premise; we record its absence rather than a weakened version. Third, we did not
obtain a version of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} for nonlinear observation
operators: the proof uses linearity essentially, at $O(m+h)=O(m)+O(h)$.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
The three conjectures of the series remain open and are not advanced here:
modular identifiability of monoidal composites; structured recovery of a
Hamiltonian in a fixed dictionary under a restricted-injectivity condition; and
hierarchical propagation of validated uncertainty through composition. To these
we add Conjecture~\ref{conj:transfer}, and the question of whether the
cohomological gap of Section~\ref{sec:global} admits a formulation in the
language of Definition~\ref{def:obs}, which would unify all three mechanisms
rather than the two that Section~\ref{sec:kernel} unifies; we have no such
formulation.

\section{Conclusion}

The three manuscripts prove different theorems about the same gap.
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} shows that two of their central negative results are
one result about a linear map with nonzero kernel; Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge}
supplies the categorical form of a second, independent degeneracy;
Section~\ref{sec:global} exhibits a third that neither of the first two detects;
and Proposition~\ref{prop:nofactor} turns the standing objection from gauge
constraints into a theorem. Lemma~\ref{lem:comp} is the sole positive
propagation result, and Proposition~\ref{prop:joint} bounds its scope. Claim C6
emerges unchanged and better supported.
